- SRV-10 review rounds 2–3 (residual, T3): viewers that never read can grow their own send buffers without new
  joins (every `ping` they send queues a `pong`; there is no per-socket message budget yet — SRV-25), so Σ can be
  held near the budget by evicting them one by one. What that costs other sessions is now bounded: their joins are
  refused only while the live buffers are at the budget (until the next watch evicts), and a publisher is paused only
  while its own streamed viewers hold its chunks, at most 5 s at a time. Per-session shares of the budget and a
  per-socket rate budget (SRV-25) would bound it further; revisit with SRV-19's metrics and QA-08's load test.
- SRV-11 review round 3 (low): the stall deadline is the connection's inactivity timeout, which inbound bytes reset too,
  so a client that never reads but trickles request bytes keeps a download alive until the session ends or the
  snapshot is replaced (scene memory stays bounded: the `Buffer` is shared and dropped then); and nothing caps
  concurrent downloads per token (30/min only), so one token can accumulate connections (fd/socket-buffer use, T3).
  Options: a per-viewer cap on concurrent downloads (e.g. 2 → `429`) and a stall check on `socket.bytesWritten`
  instead of idle time; revisit with SRV-21/QA-08 and verify trickling through the Railway edge in DEP-06
  `[TODO verify]`. `track()` skips a connection already destroyed when the handler runs (an async hook could
  otherwise track a download whose `close` already fired).
- SRV-14 review round 3 (low): a publisher socket replaced by a resume must not act on the session any more —
  `session.renew` checks `session.publisher === connection`; SRV-15 adds the same check to `session.close` and the
  snapshot handlers (or closes the old socket at the resume). Residual of the heartbeat's sign-of-life rule: a live
  publisher can get `4009` (and then resumes, SRV-15) only if, at once, its own streamed viewers hold its chunks
  while Σ is at the budget (so the fan-out pauses it) and its uplink completes less than one frame per 30 s interval
  (≲ 35 KiB/s with the 1 MiB chunks of §6.2; TCP keeps filling the relay's receive buffer during a pause) — a 32 MiB
  snapshot would take a quarter of an hour at that rate.
- SRV-16 review (low): `@fastify/static`/`@fastify/send` follow symbolic links, also out of `public/` — SRV-20's image
  build refuses them (task text). `connect-src 'self' wss:` lets an injected
  script open a WebSocket to any host; the relay knows its public origins (`ALLOWED_ORIGINS`), so `wss://<host>` would
  be tighter — an ADR 0006 amendment, after checking that Safari matches `'self'` for `wss:` `[TODO verify]`.
  Protocol, QA and deploy notes (CSP pointer in §10, `/j?query`, HSTS `includeSubDomains`) are in
  `docs/testing/reports/2026-10-20-server-7.md`. When textures arrive (v1.1, §7), three.js loads embedded images
  with `fetch(blob:…)`: `connect-src` then needs `blob:` (ADR 0006's "revisit CSP" follow-up).
- SRV-18 review (low): residual — a few Fastify internals log an error with its message, and the message of
  `FST_ERR_REP_ALREADY_SENT` names `request.url` (query included); only a relay bug that replies twice reaches it.
  DEP-06 reads "single startup `info` line" and "only `xff: present|absent` and the HMAC'd key": the relay prints
  `relay starting` (the figures), one `http server listening` per network interface, `relay listening` (the port),
  and its first-upgrade line also has `xffEntries`, which counts the client's own entries too — the line names and
  how to read them are in `docs/testing/reports/2026-10-20-server-8.md` for deploy.
- SRV-20 review (medium: memory; low: the rest): memory — architecture §4 counts 512 MiB of snapshots + 160 MiB of send buffers + a heap
  under 200 MiB in a 1 GiB container, but `--max-old-space-size-percentage=75` lets the heap grow to 768 MiB there;
  release/deploy choose a lower percentage, a larger container or lower budgets (report
  `docs/testing/reports/2026-10-20-server-9.md`). Candidate server task: a startup `warn` when `heapSizeLimit` plus
  both budgets exceed `process.constrainedMemory()`. `.br`/`.gz` variants of the viewer build (ADR 0006 follow-up for
  deploy) are not generated yet; `@fastify/static` already prefers them when present. The runtime stage still ships
  npm/yarn/corepack from the base image (≈ 22 MB of scanner surface, T12); removing them is optional hardening.
- SRV-10 review (low): bytes still queued on viewer sockets closed by `viewer.leave` or by a session ending are not
  counted in Σ `bufferedAmount` (those sockets leave the registry at once); they are bounded by `ws`'s 30 s
  `closeTimeout` (those sockets are not slow consumers closed by the fan-out, which terminates its own after 5 s) and are
  mostly shared chunk `Buffer`s. If SRV-19's metrics show it matters, track every closing
  viewer socket until its `close` event.
