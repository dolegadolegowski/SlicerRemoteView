# `SLOW_CONSUMER` / `4010` can now reach a publisher

- **Reported by**: server (`ws/server`, SRV-14)
- **Affects**: protocol — `docs/protocol.md` §9.2 (`SLOW_CONSUMER`), §9.3 (`4010`); slicer — reconnect handling
  (`slicer-plugin/`, plan SLC-17)
- **Severity**: low
- **Milestone**: M1
- **Protocol**: v1.0 §3.3 (watermarks), §9.1, §9.2, §9.3, §11
- **Status**: triaged (PRO-20)

## Steps to reproduce

1. A publisher creates a session and then stops reading its socket (a hung UI thread, a stalled network).
2. Replies keep queueing on the relay side: `pong`s, `viewer.count`, acks, `session.renewed`.

## Expected

§9.2 defines `SLOW_CONSUMER` as "viewer `bufferedAmount` over the hard watermark (8 MiB), stuck > 60 s, or evicted
under `MAX_TOTAL_BUFFERED_BYTES`", and §9.3 describes `4010` as "slow consumer — reconnect; the viewer gets
`scene.snapshot.ready` instead of a stream". Neither mentions a publisher.

## Actual (relay behaviour, by design)

A publisher's own send buffer is limited to 1 MiB (`PUBLISHER_BUFFER_LIMIT_BYTES` in
`relay-server/src/ws/fanout.ts`; a publisher only gets replies, a few KiB at a time): above it, at the next watch
(within 1 s), the publisher gets `error SLOW_CONSUMER` (retryable) and close `4010`; its session then enters grace
(§9.1). Without this a publisher that reads nothing would grow relay memory without bound (T3); with it all
publishers together hold at most `MAX_SESSIONS` × 1 MiB outside the viewers' `MAX_TOTAL_BUFFERED_BYTES`. The schema
allows it (`error` relay → publisher is in §11; `SLOW_CONSUMER` is in the enum).

## Notes / suggested change

- Protocol: widen §9.2/§9.3 from "viewer" to "socket", list the publisher limit (1 MiB, no soft watermark) next to
  the viewer watermarks in §3.3, and add the publisher's reaction to `4010`: reconnect and `session.resume` (within
  `PUBLISHER_GRACE_S`).
- Slicer: treat `4010` like a drop (reconnect with backoff, then `session.resume`), not as a fatal error.
- Protocol, same revision: §9.1 says the relay terminates sockets "that did not pong by the next tick". The relay
  applies it as "showed no sign of life by the next tick": a missing pong is excused one interval at a time while
  the relay itself paused the socket's reading and the socket kept sending frames, or while its send buffer shrank
  (the ping may still be queued); a dead peer is still closed within about 60 s (`relay-server/src/ws/heartbeat.ts`).
  Suggested wording: "…terminates sockets that did not pong, and showed no other sign of life, by the next tick".
