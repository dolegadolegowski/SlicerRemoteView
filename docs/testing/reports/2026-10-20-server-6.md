# A slow viewer's `pong` can wait longer than the clients' 10 s `pong` timeout

- **Reported by**: server (`ws/server`, SRV-14 review)
- **Affects**: protocol — `docs/protocol.md` §9.1 (application heartbeat), §3.3 (viewer watermarks); web — WEB-04
  (`web-viewer/`); quest — QST-06 (`quest-app/`)
- **Severity**: low
- **Milestone**: M1
- **Protocol**: v1.0 §3.3, §6.4, §9.1
- **Status**: open

## Steps to reproduce

1. A viewer on a slow link (e.g. slow 4G, ≈ 300 KiB/s) is streamed a large snapshot (§6.4): the relay forwards
   chunks to it while its `bufferedAmount` is below the 4 MiB soft watermark (§3.3), so up to ≈ 4 MiB plus one chunk
   can be queued for it on the relay, plus what the kernel and the edge proxy hold.
2. The viewer sends its application `ping` (every 15 s, §9.1). The relay answers `pong` at once, but the `pong` is
   queued behind those bytes.

## Expected

§9.1: "Clients send `ping` every 15 s; if no `pong` arrives within 10 s they close the socket and reconnect". The
relay's own rules keep such a viewer: it is reading, the fan-out defers it at 4 MiB and closes it only above 8 MiB
or after 60 s at or above 4 MiB, and the relay's WS heartbeat excuses a ping queued behind data the viewer is taking
(`relay-server/src/ws/heartbeat.ts`).

## Actual

Below ≈ 400 KiB/s (4 MiB / 10 s) the relay's `pong` reaches the viewer more than 10 s after its `ping`, so the
client closes a live, reading socket itself and reconnects (rejoin, then `scene.snapshot.ready` and an HTTP fetch).
No relay rule is involved; the relay cannot reorder its queue (WebSocket frames on one connection are in order).

## Notes / suggested change

- Web/Quest: count any inbound frame (text or binary, e.g. snapshot chunks) as liveness for the `pong` timeout, and
  close only after 10 s without any inbound data — while a backlog delays the `pong`, the frames ahead of it keep
  arriving.
- Protocol: say so in §9.1 ("if nothing arrives within 10 s of a `ping`"), or lower the soft watermark for streamed
  viewers so that a `pong` is never more than ≈ 10 s behind at the slowest supported link (a server change, SRV-10,
  which would defer more viewers to the HTTP fetch).
