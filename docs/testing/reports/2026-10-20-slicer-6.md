# Heartbeat: the 10 s `pong` deadline of §9.1 / NFR-21 cuts a live snapshot upload on a slow uplink

- **Reported by**: slicer (`ws/slicer`, SLC-26, on top of `fd5c060`)
- **Affects**: release — `docs/protocol.md` §9.1, `docs/requirements.md` NFR-21, `docs/adr/0009-*.md` Decision 4
- **Severity**: medium (a presenter on a hospital Wi-Fi or a tethered phone loses the session while the first scene uploads)
- **Milestone**: M1
- **Protocol**: v1.0 §9.1 (the wording below is a proposal; no wire change)
- **Status**: triaged (SLC-40: the v1.1 §9.1 deadline, `B ≤ 4 MiB`, out-of-cycle `ping`); release 2026-10-27-protocol-4

## Steps to reproduce

1. Run `slicer-plugin/tests/test_transport.py` and `tests/test_snapshot.py::test_snapshot_heartbeat_survives_a_throttled_uplink`
   with the transport's old rule (`min_uplink=1e15` makes it the literal §9.1) against `slicer-plugin/tests/throttle_proxy.mjs`
   (a TCP proxy limiting the bytes per second towards the relay), or follow `docs/testing/slicer.md` §2 step 16.
2. Publish a 1.4 MB snapshot with the uplink limited to 500 KB/s … 60 KB/s.

## Expected

An upload that is making progress is not a dead connection.

## Actual

§9.1 says "if no `pong` arrives within 10 s they close the socket". The relay reads the publisher's one TCP stream in
order, so it answers a `ping` only after every byte written before it. The WebSocket client's `send()` returns once its own
write buffer is nearly empty; the kernel's send buffer, the network and the relay's receive buffer then hold several MB
(4.3 MB measured on a Linux loopback pair) ahead of the `ping`. At 1 Mbit/s that backlog needs more than 30 s, so the
literal rule closed a working connection 10 s after the first `ping` in every run (1.9 s with the scaled timers; table in
`docs/research/slicer-heartbeat.md`). Reconnecting does not help: the session resumes with a re-upload (no resume before SLC-17).

## Notes / suspected cause

SLC-26 widens the deadline in the Slicer transport only: `pong_timeout + unread / 125 000 B/s`, where `unread` is what was
written since the last answered `ping`, less what a 1 Mbit/s link has read since, at most 8 MiB. This keeps the 10 s on an
idle connection but **loosens NFR-21's "≤ 25 s to detect a dead connection"**: a peer that reads and never answers is
detected after up to 10 s + 8 MiB / 1 Mbit/s ≈ 77 s while a large backlog is unread (it cannot be told from a slow link);
a peer that stops reading is ended after 60 s by the send timeout.

Please decide, and amend the documents so the clients follow one rule:

- §9.1 / NFR-21: "A client may extend the `pong` deadline by the time the bytes it wrote since the last answered `ping`
  need at the slowest uplink it supports; the idle bound stays 10 s." (or fix a different bound, or accept the 60 s
  send timeout as the only bound during uploads);
- ADR 0009 Decision 4 (the transport sends `ping` every 15 s and a late `pong` aborts) — add the extension;
- `web` and `quest` are viewers and upload no scenes, so the rule concerns publishers only.

## Also: Stop and Slicer exit during a slow upload do not end the session (US-03, FR-13, T1/T4)

The stop message (`session.close`) goes behind the bytes already written, which the buffers hold up to ≈ 4 MB —
about 35 s at 1 Mbit/s. `RelayTransport.stop` aborts the socket after 2 s, so the relay never reads it (reproduced with
`throttle_proxy.mjs` at 125 000 B/s: `final_sent` false, no `session.close` at the relay). The relay keeps the session in
grace (`PUBLISHER_GRACE_S`, 120 s): the code stays valid and the cached scene visible to viewers although the presenter
pressed Stop. This is not new in SLC-26 (the SLC-25 follow-up recorded it); the panel tells the presenter. Please judge
whether US-03/FR-13 allow it as a residual risk of a slow uplink, or whether the contract should have the relay end a
session on a lost publisher faster than the grace period (it cannot tell this case from a blip), or add a client-side
rule such as "no more than a few MiB written ahead of the last answered `ping`". The slicer workstream will take the
last option up in SLC-14/SLC-16 unless `release` decides otherwise.
