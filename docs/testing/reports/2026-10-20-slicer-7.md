# SRV-14: a healthy publisher uploading on a slow uplink answers the relay's WebSocket ping late — count inbound frames as liveness

- **Reported by**: slicer (`ws/slicer`, SLC-26, on top of `fd5c060`)
- **Affects**: server — `relay-server` heartbeat (SRV-14: "WS-level ping every 30 s, terminate sockets with no pong by the next tick")
- **Severity**: medium (the relay would end the session of a presenter whose chunks are still arriving)
- **Milestone**: M1
- **Protocol**: v1.0 §9.1 (relay WS ping; no wire change)
- **Status**: fixed (8cf06b8, SRV-27); closing run passed 2026-10-29 (see below)

## Steps to reproduce

1. A publisher on a 1 Mbit/s uplink (`slicer-plugin/tests/throttle_proxy.mjs`) uploads a 6.5 MB snapshot.
2. The relay's WS ping travels down the free downlink and reaches the publisher at once; its library answers
   automatically. The `pong` frame is written to the same TCP stream as the chunks, behind the several MB the publisher
   has already written — it reaches the relay 30 s or more later.
3. With "terminate if no pong by the next tick" (30–60 s of silence) the relay terminates the socket, although a chunk
   arrives every ~8 s.

## Expected

A socket from which frames keep arriving is alive.

## Actual

Not verified against `relay-server` (not on `main` yet); the §9.1 wording and the `ws` README pattern (`isAlive` reset
only by `'pong'`) would produce this. The same holds for any publisher or viewer whose uplink is saturated by something else.

## Notes / suspected cause

Proposal for SRV-14: mark the socket alive on **any** inbound frame (message, ping, pong), not only on a WS `pong`, and add a
test with a publisher that sends chunks slowly while its WS `pong` is delayed past two ticks (fake timers): the socket stays.
The application `ping` is answered in order after the chunks before it, which is fine; the chunk inter-arrival timeout of
120 s is not a problem at 1 Mbit/s (a 1 MiB chunk takes ~8.4 s). See `docs/research/slicer-heartbeat.md` ("Not verified"),
`docs/testing/reports/2026-10-20-slicer-6.md` for the client side.

## Status update, 2026-10-29 (closing run against `relay-server` on `main`)

SRV-27 (report `2026-10-27-server-2`) excuses a missed pong one 30 s interval at a time when a publisher sent a
complete frame since the last tick. The run below checked it against the real relay rather than the stub.

Setup:

- `relay-server` from `main` (built `dist/`), `NODE_ENV=production`, `LOG_LEVEL=debug`, the real 30 s heartbeat
  (`RELAY_TEST_FAST_TIMERS` does not shorten it).
- `slicer-plugin/tests/throttle_proxy.mjs` at 125,000 B/s (1 Mbit/s).
- The slicer's real client and transport through `test_snapshot.upload()`, with the client's default heartbeat
  (`ping` every 15 s, 10 s `pong` deadline extended while uploading).
- A 6,728,080 B quantised snapshot in 7 chunks of 1 MiB (synthetic spheres, `FakeScene`; no patient data), started
  about 3.5 s before a relay tick so that the relay's ping arrives while Slicer's send buffer is full.
- In an isolated sandbox, the client and the proxy on its loopback interface (the relay listens on every interface):
  `node relay-server/dist/index.js` with the variables above and `PORT=0`; `node slicer-plugin/tests/throttle_proxy.mjs
  --target 127.0.0.1:<relay port> --rate 125000`; then, from `slicer-plugin/tests`, `upload("ws://<proxy address>/ws",
  scene, timeout=180)` of `test_snapshot.py` with the `.venv` Python.

Two runs, the same result:

- `scene.snapshot.ack` arrived 56 s after the upload began. The client reported no drop. The relay closed nothing but
  the final `1000` after `session.close`: no `4009`, no `1006`, and no warning or error line.
- The relay's ping went out at a tick. Its pong reached the relay 31.5 s later, behind the fourth chunk, and so after
  the next tick.
- In between, three complete chunks arrived, so the relay kept the socket (SRV-27). The rule of SRV-14 would have
  closed it there with `4009`. That conclusion follows from the timing; no run disabled the exemption.
- No session code or token appeared in the relay's log.

Caveat: the pong was late by only 1.5 s past one tick. The exemption held for one tick, not for several in a row (that
would take a snapshot of 20 MiB or more at 1 Mbit/s). Manual §2 step 16 repeats the run from Slicer through the same
proxy.
