# §9.1: a publisher's inbound frames count as life for the relay's WebSocket ping (SRV-27)

- **Reported by**: server (`ws/server`, SRV-27)
- **Affects**: protocol — `docs/protocol.md` §3.3 (row "Relay WS ping"), §9.1; `shared/ts/constants.ts`
  (comment on `RELAY_PING_INTERVAL_S`); release — `docs/requirements.md` FR-21 (tagged "non-negotiable"),
  NFR-21; slicer — report `2026-10-20-slicer-7` (fixed on the relay side)
- **Severity**: low (no wire change; the relay closes fewer sockets than the text allows) — but FR-21's tag
  makes it a release decision
- **Milestone**: M2
- **Protocol**: v1.0 §3.3, §9.1, §9.3
- **Status**: triaged (PRO-20: FR-21 accepted; release, 2026-10-27-protocol-4)

## Steps to reproduce

1. A publisher uploads a 6.5 MB snapshot on a 1 Mbit/s uplink: a 1 MiB chunk every ~8.4 s. Its pong to the
   relay's WS ping waits behind its own chunks (TCP keeps the order) for more than one 30 s tick.
2. A socket stops answering WS pings but keeps sending text or binary frames.

## Expected (protocol v1.0)

§9.1: "The relay sends a WebSocket-level ping every 30 s and terminates sockets that did not pong by the next
tick (max 60 s of silence)"; §3.3: "terminate if no pong by the next tick"; FR-21/NFR-21: "a connection is
considered dead when no pong arrives before the next ping tick".

## Actual (relay behaviour, by design — `relay-server/src/ws/heartbeat.ts`, SRV-27)

1. A missed pong is excused, one 30 s interval at a time, when a **publisher** sent a complete text or binary
   frame since the last tick, or when the socket's send buffer shrank (the peer is reading; the ping may still
   be queued behind older data); the ping stays outstanding and a late echo counts. The publisher of step 1
   keeps its session.
2. A viewer's frames do not count: it sends small text frames only, which never hold its pong back for a tick,
   and a viewer that kept sending but read nothing would otherwise keep its slot and the relay's queues for it
   (kernel ones included) for good.
3. A socket without such a frame, an echo or a shrinking send buffer is closed `4009` at the second tick: at
   most about 60 s. Pongs that echo no ping and the client's own WS pings are no sign of life.
4. A publisher that keeps sending frames but reads nothing is not closed by the heartbeat; it is bounded by
   the relay's 1 MiB limit on its send buffer (`SLOW_CONSUMER`, `4010`), plus what the kernel queues for it.
5. Every §9.1-compliant client sends an application `ping` every 15 s: for a compliant publisher the relay's
   WS pong is no longer needed to stay connected; a dead downlink is detected by the client's own `pong`
   timeout.

This supersedes the explanatory clause in the last bullet of `2026-10-20-server-5.md`, which still names the
fan-out's pause as a condition; its suggested wording ("showed no other sign of life") stands, made precise
below.

## Notes / suggested change

- Protocol §9.1: "…terminates sockets that did not pong by the next tick unless, since the previous tick, the
  publisher sent a text or binary frame or the socket's send buffer shrank (max 60 s without an echo, reading
  or, from the publisher, a frame)". §3.3 row "Relay WS ping": "terminate if no pong, no publisher frame and
  no draining by the next tick". The same in the comment on `RELAY_PING_INTERVAL_S` and in FR-21/NFR-21.
- Release: FR-21 is tagged "non-negotiable"; please confirm the change above (or reject it, and the relay goes
  back to SRV-14's rule, under which the publisher of step 1 is closed `4009`).
- Liveness is then split by direction: the relay detects dead or silent uplinks, and a client detects a dead
  downlink by its own `pong` timeout (§9.1).
- Slicer: nothing to change. Its own 1 Mbit/s floor (`MIN_UPLINK_BYTES_PER_S`) binds well before the relay's
  (≈ 34 KiB/s of payload with §6.2's 1 MiB chunks, ≈ 43 KiB/s while the fan-out pauses it). Slicer or QA may
  close `2026-10-20-slicer-7` after a run through `slicer-plugin/tests/throttle_proxy.mjs` against a relay
  with the heartbeat on.
