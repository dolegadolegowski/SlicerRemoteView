# A per-socket message budget: `RATE_LIMITED` and `4006` beyond join and create (SRV-25)

- **Reported by**: server (`ws/server`, SRV-25)
- **Affects**: protocol — `docs/protocol.md` §3.3 (limits), §8.1 (publisher pacing), §9.1 (pings), §9.2
  (`RATE_LIMITED`, `SLOW_CONSUMER`), §9.3 (`4006`, `4010`, `1008`); slicer — a publisher over its budget is
  closed, not throttled (SLC-16 must pace split updates); web and quest — handling of `RATE_LIMITED` on an
  established socket; qa — load tests must stay within the budgets or raise them
- **Severity**: medium (a wire behaviour the protocol does not define; a publisher above the ceiling below is
  closed)
- **Milestone**: M1
- **Protocol**: v1.0 §3.3, §8.1, §9.1, §9.2, §9.3
- **Status**: triaged (PRO-25: wording sync round 2, not yet in protocol.md)

## Steps to reproduce

1. A client floods a bound socket with text frames, binary frames, WebSocket pings or unsolicited pongs.
2. A client sends WebSocket pings and reads nothing.
3. A client sends a message in more than 64 fragments.

## Expected (protocol v1.0)

§9.2 defines `RATE_LIMITED` as "join/create attempts exceeded; `details.retryAfterS`", closing `4006`, and
`SLOW_CONSUMER` for a socket whose send buffer passes the watermarks of §3.3; the limits table of §3.3 lists no
limit on how much an established socket may send, nor on fragments; WebSocket pings from clients are not
mentioned (browsers cannot send them, §3.3 row "Application heartbeat").

## Actual (relay behaviour, by design — `relay-server/src/ws/budget.ts`, `ws/endpoint.ts`, SRV-25)

1. Every text frame, binary frame and WebSocket ping or pong that passes the size and role checks (`1009`,
   `1008`, unchanged) is charged, before it is parsed, to a byte-weighted token bucket of its socket: its bytes
   plus a per-frame cost. Buckets (refill per second / burst / per frame): not yet bound 16 KiB / 512 KiB /
   512 B, viewer 8 KiB / 64 KiB / 512 B, publisher 4 MiB / 4 MiB / 32 KiB (at most ≈ 128 frames a second),
   publisher snapshot chunks 32 MiB / 161 MiB / 16 KiB (two whole 64 MiB snapshots in 64 KiB chunks at once).
2. The resulting ceiling for a publisher: about 4 MiB/s of text, counting 32 KiB per message — one maximal
   (256 KiB, §3.3) update every 100 ms (§8.1), or three every 250 ms (a split change), never meets it; two
   maximal updates every 100 ms are closed after ≈ 2.2 s. Updates within the §7.5 profiles (≤ 120 visible
   nodes, ≈ 30 KiB) are far below it. Chunks: 32 MiB/s after the 161 MiB burst.
3. A **publisher** whose frame the bucket cannot pay gets `error RATE_LIMITED{retryAfterS}` (retryable) and close
   `4006` at once: a dropped `begin`, `end`, chunk or update would leave a snapshot or the versions half applied
   (§6.2, §8.1).
4. A **viewer's** or a not yet bound socket's frame the bucket cannot pay is dropped unparsed; the socket gets
   `error RATE_LIMITED{retryAfterS}` (retryable, no `replyTo`). `retryAfterS` covers its debt and one more small
   frame; frames it sent before the warning arrived add to the debt, so a socket still limited once
   `retryAfterS` has passed is warned again, with a new time. A socket that sends on until it owes a second
   burst gets `RATE_LIMITED` and `4006`.
5. The relay answers a client's WebSocket ping itself, only within that budget and only while the pong leaves the
   socket's send buffer within the 8 MiB hard watermark; otherwise `error SLOW_CONSUMER` and `4010` — also for a
   bound publisher, and for a socket not bound yet, which is held to the same watermark at every frame.
6. A message of more than 64 fragments is closed `1008` by the WebSocket library before it is decoded (each
   fragment costs the relay a frame, the budget charges the message once).
7. Frames of a socket that is closing are charged too; once it owes a second burst it is no longer read, and it
   ends by the relay's close timer (≥ 5 s) — the close code still reaches it.

## Notes / suggested change

- Protocol §9.2: widen `RATE_LIMITED` to "join/create attempts exceeded, or the socket's message budget (§3.3)
  exhausted; `details.retryAfterS`", and say that a viewer's frames are dropped until `retryAfterS` (sending on
  closes `4006`) while a publisher is closed `4006` at once. Widen `SLOW_CONSUMER` to "the socket's send buffer
  passed the watermarks of §3.3 — also by pongs to its own WebSocket pings, and before the handshake".
- Protocol §3.3 limits table: a row "Per-socket message budget | bytes + a per-frame cost; per second / burst as
  above | relay (`RATE_LIMITED`, then `4006`)", and a row "Fragments per message | 64 | relay (`1008`)".
- Protocol §8.1, a publisher constraint next to the debounce guidance: "Keep sustained text below ≈ 4 MiB/s,
  counting 32 KiB per message (one maximal update every 100 ms), and chunks below 32 MiB/s, counting 16 KiB per
  chunk, after a 161 MiB burst; the relay closes a publisher above it `4006`."
- Protocol §9.1: "WebSocket pings from clients are answered within the socket's budget and the 8 MiB send
  buffer (`4010` beyond it)."
- Slicer: SLC-16 splits changes above 1,000 items or 256 KiB; keep the split messages of one change within the
  ceiling above (three maximal messages every 250 ms fit). Send each message as one WebSocket frame. The panel
  shows "Too many sessions were started from this network" for any `RATE_LIMITED` (`panel.py`, `ws/slicer`):
  after a budget close (no `replyTo`) it should say the relay limited this presenter's traffic; the transport
  already resumes after `4006` and `retryAfterS`.
- Clients that answer the relay's WebSocket ping: a pong sent while the socket is over its budget is not counted,
  and the heartbeat (§9.1) may close the socket `4009` at the next tick — only a client already over its budget.
- Web and Quest: on `RATE_LIMITED` with no `replyTo`, pause sending for `retryAfterS`. A compliant viewer (one
  `ping` per 15 s, a `scene.request` per gap) never meets its budget.
- QA: load tests that send faster than a client may (QA-08) are closed `4006`; run them against a relay built
  with raised budgets (`buildApp`'s `budgets` option) or stay within the numbers above.
