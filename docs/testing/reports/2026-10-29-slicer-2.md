# Server plan: the publisher's pong bound is ≤ 70 s since SLC-40, not SLC-26's ≈ 77 s

- **Reported by**: slicer (`ws/slicer`, SLC-40 review)
- **Affects**: server — `docs/plan/server.md` (the SRV-27 review note under SRV-25's follow-ups)
- **Severity**: low (a figure in a plan; no code depends on it)
- **Milestone**: M3b
- **Protocol**: v1.1 §9.1
- **Status**: open

## Steps to reproduce

1. Read `docs/plan/server.md`, the follow-up "SRV-27 review (medium, for SRV-25; still open …)".
2. Compare with `slicer-plugin/RemoteView/remoteview_core/transport.py` on `ws/slicer` after SLC-40.

## Expected

The note bounds the time a publisher's `pong` may wait behind its own bytes by the rule the Slicer transport follows
now: protocol v1.1 §9.1, `10 s + min(60 s, B / 125,000 B/s)` with `B ≤ 4 MiB` (the transport's `WRITE_AHEAD_BYTES`,
`MAX_PONG_EXTENSION_S`), so at most 70 s and ≈ 44 s with 4 MiB ahead.

## Actual

It cites `MAX_UNREAD_BYTES` at `MIN_UPLINK_BYTES_PER_S` and "≈ 77 s" — SLC-26's 8 MiB cap, which SLC-40 removed
(`MAX_UNREAD_BYTES` no longer exists).

## Notes / suspected cause

The plan predates SLC-40. With `B ≤ 4 MiB` a `pong` waits behind at most ≈ 34 s of bytes at 1 Mbit/s, so excusing one
`ping` for two of the relay's 30 s ticks already covers it; three or four ticks are more than the Slicer needs. The
`server` workstream owns the file; nothing in the Slicer changes.
